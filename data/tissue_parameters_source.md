`tissue_parameters.csv` stores PRESTUS' 1 MHz coefficients `alpha_coeff = alpha_500kHz / 0.5^alpha_power` and, for skull and water, `absorption_coeff = beta_500kHz / 0.5^alpha_power`; brain absorption is stored as `absorption_fraction` = β / α

Footnotes as in Table 1 (27.10.26): † value from a non-human sample; ‡ value from non-skull bone; ♠ low skull attenuation used as a proxy for maximal skull absorption; ♦ absorption bounded by the attenuation coefficient (α = β).

Brain absorption is specified as a fraction of attenuation (29 / 77 / 100 % for LOW / reference / HIGH; Table 1 of the 28.10.26 draft), skull and water absorption as a coefficient. In the CSV, brain rows leave `absorption_coeff` empty and skull and water rows leave `absorption_fraction` empty. Heat deposition uses the absorption coefficient β (PRESTUS `thermal.absorption_model: coefficient`, `absorption_coeff` in dB/(MHz^y cm), y as for attenuation), because PRESTUS applies one absorption model per run. `code/functions/itrusst_resolve_absorption.m` converts the brain fraction with the attenuation it is combined with (β = fraction × α), after every property override. At the same level, the fractions reproduce the table's brain coefficients: 0.29 × 0.154 = 0.045 and 0.77 × 0.30 = 0.231 dB/cm. The skull coefficients imply fractions of 0.26 / 0.23 / 0.34 (LOW / reference / HIGH). Until 2026-10-28, brain absorption was a coefficient (LOW 0.045, reference 0.231, HIGH = attenuation) and the CSV fraction column (brain 0.73 / 0.865 / 1, skull 0.16 / 0.58 / 1) was unused.

Because brain absorption is a fraction, it follows the attenuation it is combined with. In the one-at-a-time sweep (other properties at reference), the brain absorption bounds are 0.29 × 0.30 = 0.087 and 1.0 × 0.30 = 0.30 dB/cm, and the brain attenuation bounds carry the reference fraction: 0.77 × 0.154 = 0.119 and 0.77 × 0.493 = 0.380 dB/cm. In setup 1 and the 'top' configuration, brain absorption equals attenuation (0.493); in setup 2 and the 'bottom' configuration it is 0.29 × 0.154 = 0.045. The skull coefficient is capped at 100 % of attenuation (`itrusst_resolve_absorption.m`); skull HIGH absorption (4.02) equals skull LOW attenuation, so the cap never binds. Skull LOW attenuation (4.02) is the low end of whole-skull attenuation in White et al. (2006), which also serves as a proxy for the high end of skull absorption (HIGH, 4.02). White et al. report 53 Np/m (4.60 dB/cm) at 0.548 MHz and a least-squares power-law exponent of 1.47 across 0.272 / 0.548 / 0.840 MHz; scaling the 0.548 MHz value with b = 1.47 gives 4.02 dB/cm at 500 kHz. Simulations run at 500 kHz with y = 1 for skull, so the 500 kHz value is kept and the 1 MHz coefficient is rescaled with y = 1 (`alpha_coeff` = `absorption_coeff` = 4.02 / 0.5 = 8.04 dB/(MHz cm)) rather than with y = 1.47. An earlier version of the 2026-10-16 table gave 3.22 dB/cm, which does not match this conversion, and the 2026-10-16 table gave 4.03 dB/cm (b = 1.43 for attenuation, 1.46 for absorption). Skull LOW (1.05) and reference (1.40) are the bottom and top of White et al. (1978; 2.1–2.8 dB/cm at 1 MHz). Skull LOW was 1.35 (longitudinal-wave absorption of Pinton et al., 2012; 2.7 dB/cm at 1 MHz) until 2026-10-19. Brain LOW absorption (0.045) and reference absorption (0.231) are the bovine gray- and white-matter absorption of Lyons & Parker (1988; 0.012 and 0.064 Np/cm, b = 1.21 and 1.27); brain reference absorption was 0.093 (Goss et al., 1979) until 2026-10-27. Brain LOW attenuation (0.154) is the bovine white/gray-matter attenuation of Lyons & Parker (1988; 0.040 Np/cm at 1 MHz, b = 1.17); it was 0.236 (Bamber, 1981) until 2026-10-27. Skull reference conductivity was 0.315 until 2026-10-27.

Skull reference sound speed, density and attenuation (2550 m/s, 1775 kg/m³, 6.0 dB/cm) are a project decision: the mean of the cortical (2800 m/s, 1850 kg/m³, 4 dB/cm) and trabecular (2300 m/s, 1700 kg/m³, 8 dB/cm) bone values of Aubry et al. (2022). The 2026-10-08 table used the cortical values alone; the other skull reference values follow the table.
